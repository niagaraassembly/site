Methods

How we research the news

Methods · Updated 10 October 2026

Every story on our news pages has passed through one research system, kept in the repository niagaraassembly/na-research. This page explains how that system works, from the first search to the story you read here, and what each label on a story means.

The short version: AI research agents find and record news, every finding is reviewed under a person’s authority, approved findings are merged into our research database once a week, and only then can an editor clear them for publication. Nothing skips a step, and the steps never run in reverse.

The principles

  • Nothing is invented. A missing fact is shown as missing. A scan that finds nothing is a valid result.
  • Every fact keeps its source. Each claim records where it came from, so it can be checked again later.
  • People approve; scores don’t. The system scores matches and sources to help reviewers, but a score never approves anything.
  • Disagreement is kept, not resolved by guessing. When sources differ, we show each value with its source. We never average them or pick one.
  • Everything is auditable. Decisions are added to a log, not written over. Rejected and conflicting records stay on file.

From search to story

A finding moves through eight steps:

  1. Discover. Research agents run regional searches most days, with a final scan on Saturday before the weekly review. Anyone on the team can also submit a single link by hand. Both paths use the same intake, duplicate checks and record formats.
  2. Write a dossier. Each article we keep gets one permanent file, with an ID like ART-2026-000027. It holds a factual summary, why the story matters to the region, the organizations named, the events and individual claims it contains, conflicts, and open questions. We never store a full copyrighted article, only short paraphrases and pointers to the source.
  3. Break it into records. The dossier is split into structured rows: the article, each organization mentioned, each event (an expansion, a contract, a closure), each individual claim (a dollar figure, a job count, a date), and relationships between organizations.
  4. Match identities. Each organization mentioned is compared with the companies already in our database, so one company isn’t recorded twice under two names. Matching weighs the name, known aliases, website, location, parent company and line of business. We never match on a name alone when two organizations could share it.
  5. Review. Every record is reviewed and given one of eight decisions (listed below). A person makes each decision, or authorizes an agent to record it, and the log names who recorded it. Decisions are appended to a single log; review never edits the original evidence.
  6. Follow up. Records marked for follow-up or with conflicting evidence become narrow research tasks, answered before the record can move on.
  7. Weekly integration. On Saturday, only records a person approved, and whose company identity is settled, are merged into the research database. The merge is first run as a dry run and checked, then proposed for review rather than merged automatically.
  8. Publish. Only after integration can an editor clear a story for the public. That clearance is a separate decision, made by a person, and it is the only thing that puts a story on this site.

A record with no decision is unreviewed, not rejected, and never appears on this site.

How a story is marked

Each published story carries four markings that describe it, plus the publishing decision itself. They answer different questions, so they are kept apart.

Section: where the story belongs

Investment and industryDirect investment, contracts and closures.
In developmentStated intentions and applications. Nothing is built or confirmed yet.
Corridor newsReal regional news outside direct investment and hiring, such as research grants or base-support contracts. Good news, but never added to an investment or jobs total.

Some findings are excluded altogether and never published.

Stage: how real the underlying fact is

One badge per story, from earliest to latest: Stated intent, Application only, Announced, Plan approved, Contract or award, Under way, Completed. A story we know only from a report, with no stage confirmed, is marked Reported.

Flags: what the reader should know

Sources disagreeSources give different values. Each value is shown with its source.
Not disclosedA fact that matters, such as a location or a local share, was not stated by any source.
Being confirmedAn open research task is checking this point.
Company-reportedThe only source is the company itself.
Secondary listingFound in a secondary listing; we have not read the original notice.
Contract ceilingThe figure is the most a contract could be worth, not money committed or spent.
Identity being confirmedWhich company this is has not been settled.
Research fundingA research award, not a production or hiring commitment.
Not counted in production totalsKept out of any regional production figures.

Explainer and sources

The line under each headline is the reviewer’s note written for readers, one or two sentences on what the story does and doesn’t establish. Where it is blank, we show nothing rather than generate one. Each story links its sources, marked as a primary source (a government release or regulatory filing), a company source, or a news report. When several outlets covered the same event, they are listed as extra sources on one story, not as separate stories.

What we never publish

Internal reviewer notes, the reasons behind a claim’s uncertainty, dossier prose and unreviewed records stay inside the research system.

Money and totals

Dollar figures are listed one per line, only from investment and industry stories, and only where an amount was recorded as data. Contract ceilings and corridor news are left out. We don’t add the figures together.

How this site is updated

Once a week, after the Saturday integration is merged, we export the approved stories from the research database to this site. The export reads the research data and never changes it. Each run reports which stories were added, changed or removed since the last one, and the research version it came from, so every update to this site can be traced to the research behind it.

What is planned, not yet running

A single scheduled wrapper for the research agents, so every routine runs the same way under a lock with a full run record, is specified but not yet switched on. Scans currently run as agent research sessions that each propose their changes for review. No routine merges its own work.
