Hamilton

Hamilton is where it comes together. The Hamilton Hub is where Niagara Assembly’s plans and action areas meet: an early-stage place where new services and products collide, work themselves out, and gain definition.

Planning stage. A location has not been chosen; several sites are being discussed. Everything here is subject to change.

Old Hamilton, by the working waterfront

The Hub will be in old Hamilton, not far from the industrial waterfront the city is known for, and close enough to feel it. That waterfront is where Hamilton became the steel city, and it is where the next round of change is now set to happen. We want to be near the work, and near the people who will need skills, space and tools to do it.

The waterfront next door

WhatThe detail
Port of Hamilton The largest port in Ontario: about 10 million tonnes of cargo and around 650 vessel calls a year, on 630 acres. Its cargo includes steelmaking raw materials, finished steel, and manufactured components and machinery. The port says it supports about 38,000 cargo-related jobs.
Steelport Nearly 800 acres of former Stelco land, bought by Slate Asset Management, with plans for up to 12 million square feet of mostly industrial development. Cleanup and redevelopment are expected to take about ten years, and the city’s chief planner has suggested a 20 to 25 year buildout.
Bayfront Industrial Area Strategy The City’s plan for the Bayfront, Hamilton’s largest industrial area. Of its 47 actions, six were achieved and 13 more were under way at the end of 2024.
Steelmaking ArcelorMittal Dofasco has announced a $1.8 billion move from coke ovens and blast furnaces to electric arc furnaces; the 2028 target has since slipped.

Big things are moving on the waterfront, and not on a single schedule. Whatever they become, they will need trained people, working space and good information. That is what we are gathering.

What that looks like

Picture one person’s path. A solo founder arrives with a product idea and works it through with us in Grow. They take an electronics class, and they book a bench and a storage bin to build the first prototype. They post what they need on the Board and demo the result at an event, and later they turn up in Solo. No one of those things is remarkable. What matters is that they happen in one place, and shape one another.

Why a cluster

One idea we are carrying forward is that regions get strong through density. The Leamington and Kingsville area of Essex County is now the largest concentration of greenhouses in North America, the product of many specialised operations choosing to be near each other. We think an industrial region can be built the same way, through specialisation and coordination. The Hub is where that starts.

Not the first time

Hamilton has gathered makers and learners in ordinary buildings before. The Hamilton and Gore Mechanics’ Institute was founded in 1839 as a private lending library specialising in science and technology, and the 1900 mills of the Cotton Factory in the north end still stand, now used for making and studios. A place to learn and make is very much a Hamilton idea.
