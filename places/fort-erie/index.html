Fort Erie

Fort Erie is where Ontario meets Buffalo, at the foot of the Peace Bridge. It is the natural place for a foothold on the Niagara River, and the place we most want to learn how the border really works for a small maker.

Aspirational. We have no presence in Fort Erie yet. This page says why we want relationships, partnerships and resources there, and what we hope to build with them.

The crossing

The Buffalo and Fort Erie Public Bridge Authority, which runs the Peace Bridge, calls it “the premier Canada/U.S. international border crossing”. On the American side of it sits a gateway that Invest Buffalo Niagara says handled nearly $79 billion in U.S.–Canada freight in 2025, across seven international ports of entry. For a small maker moving parts, prototypes or finished goods in either direction, the border is not abstract. It is brokers, paperwork and timing, and most of us have never been shown how it works.

Why Fort Erie

ReasonWhat it means for us
Facing Buffalo The shortest path between an Ontario network and a New York one. A place where people from both sides can meet halfway.
Cross-border know-how Customs brokers, carriers and the Bridge Authority carry knowledge that small makers rarely see: how goods are classified, what paperwork moves them, how long it takes and what it costs. We would like to learn it and share it plainly.
Incentives that exist The Niagara Gateway Community Improvement Plan covers Fort Erie, Niagara Falls, Port Colborne, Thorold and Welland. It offers property-tax-based grants of 40% to 100% for five or ten years, depending on location, plus relief on development charges for high-scoring projects. Whether a given project qualifies is a question for the municipality.
Land for logistics and light industry The Gateway zone has roughly 2,000 hectares of designated employment land across those five municipalities, and Niagara’s own materials point to Fort Erie for cross-border trade, warehousing, logistics and light industry.
A reason to plan for change The three countries’ trade agreement, CUSMA, went into its joint review on 1 July 2026 without being renewed in its current form; it stays in force, with annual reviews. Terms will keep moving. A region that shares a river and a supply chain will still be a region.

What we would like to build

  • A place to meet. A small presence for cross-border meetups, talks and tours, where Ontario and New York makers can find each other.
  • Plain-language border guides. Written with brokers and shippers who know the process: how a small shop sends a sample, a batch or a part across, and what to expect.
  • Sites and buildings. Conversations about employment land and buildings for storage, staging and light industry, alongside our own space offerings.
  • Relationships with the Town and the Region. To understand which incentives and programs apply to a small, member-run project.
  • Resources. Introductions, advice and, over time, shared services for members who sell or source across the river.

Help shape it

If you ship across the border, work in customs or logistics, own or know a suitable building, or work in municipal development, we would like to hear from you. See also Greater Buffalo, on the other side of the bridge, and Hamilton, where we begin.
