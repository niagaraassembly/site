Erie, PA

Erie, Pennsylvania is the reachable gateway to the rest of the Midwest: a Lake Erie port city on I-90, about a hundred miles from both Buffalo and Cleveland, where the road, the rail and the water all turn west. It is close enough to Hamilton and Buffalo to be part of one corridor, and it opens onto the Great Lakes heartland beyond.

Aspirational. We have no presence in Erie yet. This page says why we want relationships, partnerships and resources there, and what we hope to build with them. Erie is in Pennsylvania, which the Census counts as Northeast; we call it a gateway because of the routes that lead west from it.

What is in Erie

Erie’s manufacturing is more varied than its reputation. The region makes more than a tenth of the nation’s plastics, and its steel and plastics plants are described as smaller and more agile than the giants that once dominated. Locomotive assembly (Wabtec), health care, insurance and universities are the other big employers.

GroupOrganizations
Port and freight The Erie-Western PA Port Authority, and Foreign-Trade Zone 247, which covers designated locations in northwest Pennsylvania.
Economic development The Erie Regional Chamber and Growth Partnership, which leads innovation and entrepreneurship work in Erie County alongside Penn State Behrend.
Universities Penn State Behrend, whose Plastics Engineering Technology program is a well-known talent pipeline, and which opened a $16.5 million Advanced Manufacturing and Innovation Center; Gannon University; Mercyhurst University; Edinboro University.
Startups and prototyping The Erie Technology Incubator at Gannon; Behrend’s Innovation Commons for product design and prototyping; and the Northwest Pennsylvania Innovation Beehive Network, a collaboration among the universities and the county library. Ben Franklin Technology Partners of Central and Northern Pennsylvania run the Erie TechCelerator.
Manufacturers Wabtec’s locomotive plant, and a large cluster of plastics, tooling and molding companies.

Ontario and Pennsylvania

In June 2026, Ontario Premier Doug Ford and Pennsylvania Governor Josh Shapiro signed a memorandum of understanding in Toronto to increase collaboration in areas including energy, critical minerals, manufacturing and life sciences, and to set up a working group. It is a government arrangement, not ours, but it shows attention to the Ontario–Pennsylvania link at the highest level.

Why we want relationships in Erie

  • A door to the Midwest. Erie is the most reachable point from which to learn how goods, people and ideas move onward along the lakes: to Cleveland, then Toledo, Detroit and Chicago.
  • Freight options. A lake port, two railroads, two interstates and a foreign-trade zone are a logistics toolkit that a small Ontario maker will not otherwise see.
  • Plastics and tooling. Nearly every product needs an enclosure, a mould or a fixture. Erie’s molding and tooling base is relevant to the products our Members develop. See Grow › Product.
  • A prototyping ecosystem like ours. Universities that lend design, market research, branding and prototyping to local startups do what we hope to do for solo founders. See Grow.
  • A shared story. A lake city that made things for a century and is finding its next century, as Hamilton is.

What we would like to build

  • Relationships along I-90. A chain of trusted hosts and partners from Hamilton to Erie, so that a Member can move work, samples and people along the corridor.
  • Freight know-how. Working with the port, carriers and brokers to describe plainly how goods move from Ontario through New York and Pennsylvania to the Midwest.
  • Manufacturing partnerships. Introductions to plastics, tooling and molding shops for Members who need enclosures and parts.
  • Training and startup exchange. Ideas and programs shared with the universities and incubators, in both directions.
  • Study visits. Tours of the port, the plastics plants and the innovation centres.
  • One map. HeavyMap reaching along the lake, so freight, sites and skills can be seen together.
