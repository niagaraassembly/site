Western New York (WNY)

Western New York is the rest of the American half of our region, from the Lake Erie shore to Rochester and the Finger Lakes. It is where the scale of the U.S. market, a deep optics, photonics and microelectronics heritage, and real gaps in the workforce all meet.

Aspirational. We have no presence in Western New York yet. This page says why we want relationships, partnerships and resources across it, and what we hope to build with them. For the Buffalo–Niagara core, see Greater Buffalo.

The picture right now

Both of these are true at once. The New York State Department of Labor reports that private-sector jobs in the Western New York region rose by 4,700, or 0.9%, to 527,900 in the year ending January 2026, while manufacturing lost 2,000 jobs. The region is not idle. It is drifting out of some of the work that makes things. That is the argument for building here.

Three pieces of Western New York

Lake Erie shore and Chautauqua County
Dunkirk, Fredonia, Jamestown		 The I-90 corridor between Buffalo and Cleveland, the Lake Erie shoreline, a county industrial development agency, and a technology incubator at SUNY Fredonia. A possible storage and shipping staging point on the U.S. side. It is a logistics idea, not an electronics cluster, and the economics need studying first.
Rochester and the Finger Lakes The New York Photonics cluster of more than 150 companies, a test, assembly and packaging facility run by AIM Photonics, and universities including RIT and the University of Rochester. Electronics manufacturers such as REDCOM in Victor and Creation Technologies in Newark and Rochester. A precision, defence-grade and photonics ecosystem far beyond what any single small maker has. A place to learn what the high standard looks like, and to find where our training and Members could complement it.
The wider upstate corridor
Syracuse, Ithaca, the Southern Tier		 The NY SMART I-Corridor tech hub, Micron’s planned Syracuse fab, a $200 million workforce program, and the microelectronics heritage of Binghamton and Endicott. Semiconductor investment on a very large scale, and a wave of demand for suppliers and skilled people that will reach Buffalo and Rochester too.

Why we want relationships across the region

  • Capability we do not have. Photonics, defence-grade assembly and semiconductors are not things a small member-run network will build. Partners in them can complement what we do, not duplicate it.
  • A common language. Standards such as IPC give shops on both sides of the border the same words for quality. See our electronics training.
  • Workforce. Universities and colleges that train engineers and technicians, and who face the same shortage we do.
  • Programs and funding. State agencies, power programs and tech-hub initiatives that support small manufacturers, and are worth understanding early.
  • Logistics. Freight, storage and shipping between the two sides of the border, and along the Lake Erie shore.
  • A long tradition. We are not the first to gather people to learn a trade. The Hamilton and Gore Mechanics’ Institute began in 1839, and in Rochester Henry Lomb and others founded the Mechanics Institute in 1885, which became the Rochester Institute of Technology.

What we would like to build

  • Study visits. Tours of Rochester’s photonics and electronics shops, and of the Buffalo makerspaces, for Ontario Members, and the same in the other direction.
  • Standards and training links. Ways for IPC-based training to be shared or recognised across the border.
  • Introductions. Between Members, Ontario shops and specialist manufacturers, so that each can find capability the others hold.
  • A logistics study. Whether the Lake Erie shore makes sense as a staging point for goods moving between Ontario and New York.
  • One map. Extending HeavyMap across the whole region, so that shops, sites and skills on both sides can be seen together.
  • Resources. Help finding programs, agencies and funders for small manufacturers.
