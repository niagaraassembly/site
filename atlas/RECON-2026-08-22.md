# Niagara lower-tier municipalities — source reconnaissance

**Reconnaissance date: 2026-08-22.** Every count, field name and licence below
was observed by querying the live endpoint on that date. Closes
[BACKLOG.md](BACKLOG.md) Tier C item 4 ("The nine Niagara lower-tier
municipalities"), and supersedes the "not yet investigated" municipal list in
[DATA-SOURCES.md](DATA-SOURCES.md).

Companion to [DATA-SOURCES.md](DATA-SOURCES.md) (Hamilton + Region, 2026-08-18)

## Headline finding

**There are not twelve municipal portals to probe. There is one shared
catalogue.** `niagaraopendata.ca` is a CKAN instance with **11 publishing
organizations and 534 datasets**, and seven of the twelve lower-tier
municipalities publish through it directly. The Tier C task as written —
"probe nine municipal portals" — was the wrong shape for the problem.

```
  329  city-of-niagara-falls      City Of Niagara Falls
   99  niagara-region             Niagara Region
   44  city-of-welland            City of Welland
   20  town-of-fort-erie          Town of Fort Erie

```

**The City of Niagara Falls publishes more open data than Niagara Region
does** — 329 datasets against 99. It was listed last in the old Tier C and is
in fact the single richest municipal publisher in the study area, Hamilton
included.

reason the Hamilton gap must be — a reader seeing sparse coverage in Pelham
would wrongly conclude Pelham has no industry.

Official Plan Schedule A carries `DESIGNAT`, `OP_ID` and a **precomputed
`AREA_SQM`** — sample value `RESORT COMMERCIAL`, 9,157.8 m². Area arriving
precomputed matters for any density or underuse ratio; it removes a
projection-sensitive calculation from our side.

Three further legacy zoning by-laws cover the pre-amalgamation townships and
are published separately: **Crowland (1538)**, **Humberstone (7069)** and
**Willoughby (B0395)**. Zoning coverage for the city is the union of four
by-laws, not one layer.

Also present, not yet inspected: Current Development Applications, Completed
Building Permits, Building Permits by Neighbourhood and Year, Development
Charges, Community Improvement Plan Areas, Urban Infill Tax Increment Grant
Area 2026, Streetscape Improvement CIP Area 2026, Address Points, Master
Street Address Guide, Subdivisions, Township Lot and Concession, MTO Buffer
Controlled Areas, Heritage Properties, Woodland Management Inventory,
2021 Census — Industry Sectors (NAICS 2017), ortho imagery for 2006 / 2013 /
2018.

**Two epochs of building footprints is the most interesting single find of
this pass.** 39,556 in 2010 against 42,247 in 2018 is a net +2,691, but the
net figure hides the useful part: differencing the two layers yields
demolitions and new construction as *physical* change, observed independently
of the NEI's *business* change. Ortho imagery at 2006/2013/2018 brackets the
same interval visually.

**Publication convention:** every Niagara Falls spatial dataset is published
twice — once in the local projection and once as a `... WGS 1984` variant.
Ingest the WGS 1984 variants. The duplicates roughly halve the apparent
catalogue size and should be filtered on discovery, not carried through.

### Niagara Region — additions beyond the NEI

ArcGIS org `services1.arcgis.com/WxiLK82TWf8W3O3f`. OGL 2.0 (Niagara Region).

Not previously catalogued, all live: **Strategic Locations for Investment**,
**Urban Area Boundaries**, **Regional Growth Centre**, **CIP Project Area**,
**Welland Canal**, **Municipal Boundaries**, **Designated Heritage
Properties**, **Address Points**, **Airports**, **NOP Area of Archaeological
Potential**, the **NES** environmental series (Inland Lakes, Wetlands,
Woodlands, Watercourses, Shoreline Areas), **Provincial Natural Heritage
System**, **Significant Woodlands**, Combined Sewage Overflows, Transit GTFS.

"Strategic Locations for Investment" is the Region's own named investment
geography and is the closest thing found to the UK atlas's strategy-zone
layer.

The NES and Natural Heritage layers are **development constraints** — they
matter as negative evidence. A parcel designated Employment that sits inside a
Provincial Natural Heritage System polygon is not the same opportunity as one
that does not, and the atlas should be able to say so.

### Niagara Peninsula Conservation Authority (NPCA)

Not previously known to publish. ArcGIS Online owner `npca.dmccullagh`,
**335 items**. Confirms the "flood risk → NPCA" lead recorded earlier as
speculative.

Relevant feature services observed: **Regulated Floodplain Extent**,
**NPCA APPROXIMATE REGULATION LANDS**, **Assessment Parcels**,
**Parcels Cityview**, Surface Water Intake Protection Zones (and an IPZ2/IPZ3
series), NPCA Administrative Boundary, Urban Areas, Conservation Properties,
`Roads_ORN`, Vegetation Less Than 5m, several watershed polygons.

**NPCA Approximate Regulation Lands is the highest-value item here.** Land
under conservation-authority regulation carries a permitting requirement that
materially changes what can be built. Combined with Regulated Floodplain
Extent it is the constraint layer the atlas currently lacks entirely.

**"Assessment Parcels" needs a licence check before any use.** MPAC bulk data
is a confirmed paid dead end; a conservation authority republishing
assessment-derived parcels may be doing so under terms that do not extend to
us. Do not ingest until the licence is established. Flagged, not assumed.

## City of Welland — resolved 2026-08-22

The 44 CKAN records registered as format `HYPERLINK` all point at
`open.welland.ca`, an **ArcGIS Hub fronting the city's own ArcGIS Server** at
`arcgisweb.welland.ca` — not ArcGIS Online. That is why the catalogue links
out instead of serving data.

**The Hub feed carries 172 datasets against CKAN's 44** — roughly four times
what the shared catalogue advertises, including the Official Plan and
Consolidated Zoning, which do not appear in CKAN at all. Discover Welland
through `https://open.welland.ca/data.json`, not through `niagaraopendata.ca`.

### The origin server is down; the Hub cache is not

Every request to `arcgisweb.welland.ca` — both the `serverprodpub` and
`serverprod` instances, at root and at individual service paths — returns:

```
{"status":"error","messages":["Could not access any server machines.
 Please contact your system administrator."]}
```

That is a server-side fault (the web adaptor cannot reach its backing
machines), not a 404, a permission refusal, or a robots restriction. The
city's own REST endpoints are therefore **unusable for live querying today**,
and any ingestion that hard-codes them will break.

The Hub's own download API at `open.welland.ca/api/download/v1/...` also
fails, with `"Downloads are not supported for this item"` (HTTP 400).

**The route that works is the ArcGIS Online Hub cache:**

```
https://opendata.arcgis.com/api/v3/datasets/{itemId}_{layer}/downloads/data?format=geojson&spatialRefId=4326
```

Eleven of twelve priority layers were retrieved this way on 2026-08-22 while
the origin was down. Cached in `scripts/.cache/welland/` (45 MB, gitignored).

|---|---:|---:|
| Building Footprints | **26,027** | 15.1 MB |
| Civic Address Points | **22,461** | 5.6 MB |
| Official Plan Schedule B | **2,598** | 14.2 MB |
| Old Zoning | 2,014 | 5.1 MB |
| Current Zoning (By-law 2017-117) | **1,981** | 5.1 MB |

**Business Licenses is listed in DCAT but is not cached and not downloadable.**
Blocked until the origin server recovers. Worth retrying — it is the one
Welland source that would carry licence status and type per business.

### Zoning is clean and directly usable

`Current Zoning` carries a proper taxonomy — `Zoning`, `ZoneCat`,
`ZoneCatSym`, `ZoneDesc`, a `Zone_doc` link to the by-law PDF, and a
precomputed `GeometrySTArea`.

```
 1193  Residential Zones          98  Institutional and Community Wellness
  229  Commercial Zones           73  Industrial Zones
  204  Open Space Zones           34  Other Zones
  142  Agricultural and Rural      7  (null) / 1 Appealed
```

**73 Industrial Zone polygons**, e.g. `G1` / "General Industrial". This is the
third municipality with authoritative industrial land, after Niagara Falls
(Official Plan Schedule A) and St. Catharines (498 Employment parcels).

**`Old Zoning` (2,014 polygons) alongside `Current Zoning` (1,981) is a second
two-epoch pair**, matching the Niagara Falls 2010/2018 building footprints.
Differencing them yields *regulatory* change — land rezoned into or out of
industrial — which is a different and arguably stronger signal than either
physical or business change.

### The Business Directory is the calibration source

923 businesses. **Zero geometry** — it is an attribute table, not a spatial
layer — but it carries `Address`, `City`, `PC`, so it is geocodable against
the 22,461 Civic Address Points already retrieved.

Fields: `Company, SectorDescription, Address, City, Prov, PC, Telephone,
Email, WebSite, FullTime, PartTime, Seasonal, Estab, Language*`.

Sector distribution includes **45 Manufacturing** and **48 Construction and
Construction Equipment**. 715 of 923 records carry a non-zero `FullTime`
count.

**This is the most methodologically useful find of the Welland pass, for two
reasons.**

1. **It is a second, independent business register for one municipality.**
   NEI 2022 records **1,299** businesses in Welland; the city's own directory
   records **923**. Two authoritative sources, same place, same rough period,
   disagreeing by ~29%. That disagreement is measurable, and measuring it
   tells us how much weight a register-versus-OSM gap actually deserves
   *before* we publish anything derived from one register alone.
2. **It carries exact employee counts where the NEI carries bands.** NEI gives
   `sizerangeemployees` ("Small (5-99 Employees)"); Welland gives integer
   `FullTime` / `PartTime` / `Seasonal`, plus `Estab` (year established).
   Welland is therefore the one municipality where the NEI's bands can be
   calibrated against real counts — which matters directly for any
   floorspace-per-employee or parcel-area-per-employee underuse ratio.

**Welland should be the engine's test municipality.** It is the only place in
the region where a scoring output can be checked against two independent
registers, exact employment figures, two epochs of zoning, and building
footprints.

## Provincial and federal — Tier B closed out

The three endpoints recorded as "reachable, catalogue never read" on
2026-08-18 were read on 2026-08-22.

### Ontario (`data.ontario.ca`, `geohub.lio.gov.on.ca`)

| Dataset | Licence | Access | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Provincially Significant Employment Zones** | **OGL – Ontario** | ZIP package, **428 KB, HTTP 200** | ★ ingest |
| Municipal Land Use Planning | OGL – Ontario | CSV + XLSX dictionary | useful, modest |
| Brownfields environmental site registry | **Not specified** | no resources attached | needs a human look |
| Ontario land parcels | **"Not applicable"** | none | closed — see below |

**PSEZ is the confirmed provincial analogue of the UK atlas's strategy-zone
layer**, and it is the Minister of Municipal Affairs and Housing's own
designation of high-economic-output employment areas across the Greater
Golden Horseshoe — which includes Niagara. It is *not* a FeatureServer; it is
an FME bulk package:

```
https://ws.gisetl.lrc.gov.on.ca/fmedatadownload/Packages/Provincially-Significant-Employment-Zones.zip
```

Different ingestion path from every other source here (download, unzip,
convert, rather than query a REST endpoint), but open-licensed and small.

**Ontario's Brownfields environmental site registry is the notional Canadian
analogue of the UK atlas's `epr.js` contaminated-land source, and it is not
currently usable.** The catalogue entry describes the post-2007 brownfields
legislation but attaches no resources and specifies no licence. The underlying
Record of Site Condition registry is a web lookup, not a feed. Recorded as a
lead requiring a human look, not as an available source.

**"Ontario land parcels" is licensed "Not applicable"** and carries ARN/PIN
identifiers linking to ownership and assessed value. This is the Teranet/MPAC
fabric behind the paywall, catalogued but not released. It **confirms and
extends the existing MPAC dead end** — there is no provincial open route to
parcel ownership or assessment either. Municipal parcel layers (Niagara Falls,
St. Catharines) remain the only open parcel geometry, and they carry no
assessed value.

### Federal (`open.canada.ca`)

Low yield for this atlas. Searches for industrial land return the 2006
National Atlas raster series (JP2/ZIP) — cartographic products, not current
vector data. Rail network results are likewise the 2006 series. StatCan
returns many NAICS-keyed tables, but they are **aggregate statistics, not
geolocated establishments**, so they cannot serve the register role the NEI
serves.

**NPRI** (National Pollutant Release Inventory) is present as "Reporting
facilities – pollutant release and transfer data", served from the ECCC data
mart at `data-donnees.ec.gc.ca`. Facility-level and geolocated, so it is a
genuine analogue of the UK atlas's EPR permit layer — but it captures only
facilities above reporting thresholds, which is a small and heavily-skewed
subset of industry. Useful as corroboration for large sites; useless as a
register.

**Net:** the federal catalogue adds one corroborating layer (NPRI) and no
register. The provincial catalogue adds one strong layer (PSEZ), one modest
one, and confirms a second dead end.

## Known weakness — Burlington and Halton

**Recorded at the user's instruction, 2026-08-23.** The western/Halton end of
the study area is the weakest part of the whole source base, and it has not
improved across three reconnaissance passes:

- **Burlington** — `opendata.burlington.ca` returns **HTTP 403**;
  `gis.burlington.ca` does not resolve; the `cityofburlington` ArcGIS Online
  org has 8 public items, none industrial, and possibly refers to Burlington
  Vermont. No DCAT feed at any usual Hub path. The 403 suggests a portal that
  exists and refuses automated requests rather than one that is absent.
- **Halton Region** — Burlington's upper tier. **Never probed at all.**

Consequence: Burlington is in the core scope and is covered by *neither* the
Hamilton nor the Niagara datasets. Any region-wide layer or score will be
weakest exactly there, and it is the one hole that cannot currently be
patched from an adjacent tier. **Next step is a human look at burlington.ca's
open data page, and a first probe of Halton Region.**

## Licence position

Materially simpler than the Hamilton side. **Every municipal dataset in this
pass is Open Government Licence 2.0**, published per-municipality but
following the same template:

```
OGL 2.0 (Niagara Region) · (Niagara Falls) · (Welland) · (Fort Erie)
        · (Lincoln) · (Niagara on the Lake) · (City of St. Catharines)
```

Brock University and a few catalogue-level datasets are CC-BY 4.0; two are
under the Statistics Canada Open Licence. All are attribution-only and
compatible with each other.

**The share-alike problem remains OSM's alone.** A derived layer built only
from OGL 2.0 municipal sources carries no copyleft obligation; the moment OSM
geometry enters the same derived feature, ODbL share-alike is in play. This is
an argument for keeping OSM-derived and municipal-derived signals separable in
the scoring output rather than merged into a single opaque number.

## What this changes for the engine

1. **The register is no longer only the NEI.** Municipal zoning, Official Plan
   designation and parcel land-use are *also* authoritative registers of what
   land is for, and they disagree with each other and with OSM in informative
   ways. Zoned industrial + designated Employment + no OSM feature + no NEI
   business is a much stronger signal than any one of those alone.
2. **Parcel geometry now exists for the two largest cities** — 79,369 parcels
   across Niagara Falls and St. Catharines. The point-in-polygon test proposed
   as the alternative to the UK atlas's 60 m centroid radius is now available
   for roughly half the region's industrial land, and the parcel is the right
   denominator for any area-based underuse ratio.
3. **Constraint layers exist and should subtract.** NPCA regulation lands,
   floodplain, Natural Heritage System, archaeological potential and MTO
   buffers all reduce what a site can become. The UK scoring engine has no
   equivalent — its signals only ever add opportunity or subtract for
   evidence of occupation. A Niagara engine needs a third category.
4. **Physical change is observable in Niagara Falls independently of the NEI**
   via the 2010/2018 footprint pair. Where the two registers agree that
   something left, confidence is high; where they disagree, that disagreement
   is itself the finding.
5. **Coverage must be a first-class UI concept, not a footnote.** Six of
   twelve municipalities have no municipal data at all. Any score computed
   region-wide will be systematically better-evidenced in Niagara Falls and
   St. Catharines, and the confidence score has to carry that.
