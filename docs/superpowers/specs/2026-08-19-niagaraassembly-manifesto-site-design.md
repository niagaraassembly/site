# Niagara Assembly — Manifesto Site Design

- Date: 2026-08-19
- Domain: `niagaraassembly.com`
- Status: design approved in conversation; implementation plan not yet written
- Companion spec: `2026-08-19-niagaraassembly-atlas-design.md` (ships **after** this)

---

## 1. What this is

A single-purpose site carrying one document — a manifesto titled **"One neighbo(u)r to another"** — with two live edges: a public **endorsement** roster and an organizer-posted **meetup** listing.

**The title is set exactly as written above and must not drift:** sentence case, and the parenthetical `(u)` is load-bearing — it carries the border in the spelling and is the thesis in six characters. Never "One Neighbour to Another", never "One Neighbor to Another", never title case.

The site exists to gather consensus for a binational industrial-renewal mission across the Niagara region, **before any entity is incorporated**. It is a statement people put their name, trade and town beside, and a set of dates where they meet in person.

## 2. What this is not

Niagara Assembly launches as a **clean lens**. The site does not mention, link to, or draw credibility from:

- **Clark** (`clark.babb.tel`, `babbworks/clark-dot-babb`) — the industrial activation protocol and Field Report 001
- **Paton Hall** (`patonhall/site`) — the Hamilton hardware hall

Both are absorbed at the level of *ideas and research*, never as named ventures. Rationale: NA asks for agreement with a mission, not endorsement of three specific pre-incorporation projects. Naming Paton Hall in particular would underwrite NA's credibility with a building that has no lease (target Oct 2026) and no incorporation (target Dec 2026) — repeating the error currently sitting in Clark's claims register, where nine "Safe to Use" claims describe a host-facility agreement that exists only as Clark-side drafts.

Re-edited background material from both projects may be brought forward **later**, once the manifesto and roster stand on their own.

## 3. The region

Hamilton at the head, west to **Brantford**, south through Norfolk to **Port Dover**, east across Haldimand to **Fort Erie** and **Niagara Falls, Ontario** — then across the river into **Western New York**, primarily **Erie and Niagara counties**, reaching as far as **Rochester**.

Named in copy as a geography, never as a national project. The border is a feature: two funding systems, two markets, one industrial inheritance.

> **Note:** an earlier brief said "Bradford" — confirmed in conversation as **Brantford**. Bradford West Gwillimbury is north of Toronto and outside the region.

## 4. Voice

Plain, concrete, unsentimental. First person plural, not corporate. Specific place names and specific, cited numbers. The register of a trade paper, not an economic development agency.

Prohibited: "unleashing potential", "world-class", "innovation ecosystem", boosterism of any kind. Informal in texture, but never narrow — copy must not specify the gender of a room, given that the thesis is a workforce shortage and half the available workforce is women.

## 5. The statement — nine movements

Target 1,200–1,800 words.

### 1 · The room in 1842 *(opening)*

Joseph Dart, a merchant, and Robert Dunbar, a Scottish mechanical engineer who studied in Canada and moved to Buffalo in 1834, build America's first steam-powered grain elevator. Mahlon Kingman, a fellow merchant, is certain it will fail — manual labour has no cheaper alternative. First year: 229,260 bushels. By 1888: over 85 million a year. Within fifteen years Buffalo has ten elevators; twenty-seven by 1863. Buffalo overtakes London and Rotterdam as the world's largest grain port, from a village of 1,800.

Opens on a story, not a diagnosis. Plants the thesis — a merchant and a machinist in one room — before naming it. **Guard against nostalgia:** the story is about risk and a skeptic being wrong, not about the good old days.

### 2 · What was lost, and it wasn't factories

The **industrial commons** (Pisano & Shih): the know-how, process capability and specialized skills held collectively across suppliers, competitors, universities and workers. Critically, it is *geographic* — it lives in people who move from firm to firm within a region.

Two consequences carry the whole document: you cannot import a commons, only reassemble one; and the unit of repair is a **region**, not a nation. This is why the Assembly crosses the border instead of picking a side of it.

### 3 · It hasn't come back — our side first

Hamilton's Bayfront Industrial Area Strategy: 47 actions, **6 achieved**, 13 initiated, 28 not started at end-2024; progress tracking paused in early 2024. Then Western New York: year to January 2026, **+4,700 private-sector jobs and −2,000 in manufacturing**.

Leading with Hamilton's failure buys the right to name Buffalo's.

### 4 · The lever nobody can buy

Up to **1.9 million of 3.8 million** US manufacturing jobs unfilled by 2033. Reshoring surveys consistently rank skilled-workforce quantity and quality *above* taxes, currency, regulation and tariffs. Governments can move money; they cannot conjure a technician.

This is the argument for why a regional body has power a legislature does not.

### 5 · The trap

Metal Craft Spinning and Stamping, a Canadian firm, investing US$1.3M in Niagara Falls, New York for up to 17 jobs — partly to minimize tariff costs. Capacity relocated thirty kilometres, not created. Two towns bidding for one plant with public money, and calling it economic development.

**Tariff handling — binding rule:** no tariff rate, dollar figure or deadline appears anywhere in the statement. As of drafting, US Section 338 proclamations (signed 20 July 2026, first-ever use of the 1930 statute for tariffs, 50% on >US$28B of Canadian goods) were due 19 August, delayed three days to 21 August, with Section 232 steel and aluminum separately at 50% and CUSMA under renegotiation. These numbers change weekly. The structural point survives every outcome and is the only form permitted: *a firm moved thirty kilometres to be on the other side of a line — true at 50%, at 25%, and at zero.* Tariffs are weather; the commons is climate. Live figures belong in a dated post, never in the statement.

### 6 · We built this before, at both ends of this arc *(the pivot)*

- **Hamilton, 1839** — the Hamilton & Gore Mechanics' Institute, in rented rooms on King Street
- **Buffalo, 1842** — a merchant and a machinist in one room *(movement 1, now paid off)*
- **Rochester, 1885** — Henry Lomb (of Bausch & Lomb) and other local businessmen found a Mechanics Institute for technical instruction of industrial workers; merged with the Rochester Athenaeum in 1891; renamed **Rochester Institute of Technology** in 1944

Founded by local tradesmen and businessmen, not by governments. Then we stopped, and the rooms emptied.

They are being reopened now, by people who worked it out without being told it was a strategy:

- **Tri-Main Center, 2495 Main Street, Buffalo** — Albert Kahn daylight factory; 600,000+ Model Ts from 1915; wartime lease to Hercules Motors and Bell Aircraft; Trico windshield wipers post-war; 650,000 sq ft abandoned; revived by Elgin Wolfe and partner in the early 1990s; today small businesses, studios, Buffalo Arts Studio — and since 2025 **Bit Haven**, a hackerspace where people teach each other to break and secure computers. One building, one century, five industries, and the newest tenant is a room for sharing technical skill.
- **Silo City, Buffalo** — Rick Smith bought the derelict grain-elevator compound in 2006; the Lyceum runs hands-on teaching, residencies partner with The Foundry and others, residents moved in from 2025. The buildings Dart's invention created, brought back.
- **The Foundry, east Buffalo** — community makerspace (metal, wood, tech, fibre labs), workforce training, mentoring, business incubation.
- **The Cotton Factory, Hamilton** — the 1900 Imperial Cotton mill, now studios and workshops.

**Editorial rule:** these are cited as *evidence the form works*, never as partners. None has agreed to anything.

The pivot's job: not *let's try something*, but **this is ours, we've done it twice, and it's already starting again.**

### 7 · What we propose

Shared tools, shared training, local procurement, local capital — with working proof, not aspiration:

- **Preston, UK** — local procurement moved 39% → 79.2%, redirecting roughly £200M; real-living-wage jobs 76% → 88%. A region deciding to spend on itself.
- **Tapestry Community Capital** — $100M+ raised from 4,500 ordinary Canadian investors through community bonds.

### 8 · The ask

Endorse: name, trade, town. Not money, not a commitment, not a membership — a declaration that you are here and this is what you can do. Then come to a meetup.

### 9 · What this is and isn't

Short and plain: not incorporated, nothing raised, no institution behind it, nothing promised. What exists is a statement, a roster, and some dates. Publishing your own limits is the cheapest credibility available and the hardest to fake.

## 6. Site architecture

**One page, two faces, one control that turns it over.** No navigation, no sub-pages at launch.

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│           One neighbo(u)r to another          │   header
├──────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│    The Statement    │    Endorsements         │   face toggle
├─────────┬───────────────────────┬────────────┤
│ MEETUP  │  Thu Sept 4, 7pm — …  │   ＋ ADD    │   ruled band (both faces)
├─────────┴───────────────────────┴────────────┤
│                                               │
│   FRONT: the statement, full width            │
│   BACK:  Voices / Everyone                    │
│                                               │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 6.1 The meetup band

Three cells, one job each: **MEETUP** (left, opens the listing), the next event (centre), **＋ ADD** (right, opens the submission form). Ruled above and below, hairline dividers, **no box and no fills** — it reads as a masthead strip belonging to the document, not a widget in front of it.

- Clicking MEETUP expands the listing **in place** (a drawer), not a page navigation.
- Each listed meetup shows date, time, venue, and an "add to calendar" link if the organizer supplied one.
- **Empty state ships on day one and matters:** the centre cell reads *"No meetups yet — post the first one"*, MEETUP greys out, and ＋ ADD is the only live control. The band becomes a single invitation rather than a broken menu.
- `/meetups` becomes its own page only when the drawer overflows. Not at launch.

### 6.2 The flip side

Sub-switch with two views:

- **Voices** *(default)* — only endorsements with published comments, in **margin attribution** layout: name, trade and town in a fixed left column (~34%), comment flowing at right. Scanning the left edge alone yields the capability census — *toolmaker, controls tech, retired millwright* — town after town. This is the map, readable before a map exists, and it absorbs wildly uneven comment lengths without looking broken.
- **Everyone** — the complete roster in **two dense columns**, `Name · Trade · Town`. 214 names should look like 214. This view also proves the binational claim without asserting it, because the towns alternate across the border as the reader goes down.
- **By town** — deferred until locations are normalized (see §7.2).

Comments collapse at ~400 characters with a "Read more +" expand.

## 7. Data model

### 7.1 `data/endorsements.json`

```json
{
  "id": "e-0142",
  "name": "Rosa Silva",
  "trade": "Toolmaker",
  "location": "Welland, ON",
  "comment": "…",
  "date": "2026-09-04"
}
```

**Email is never written to this file.** The repo is public; the contact list and the public artifact are different objects. Email goes to Kit and the private Sheet only.

`comment` is omitted entirely when not published (see §8.3).

### 7.2 Location is free text

Captured as **"City / Town / Township"**, stored exactly as typed. No predetermined list — the region's settlements cannot be enumerated thoroughly enough to be worth the friction.

**Consequence, accepted:** until normalization happens, the headline figure must be **endorsers, not towns**. "Endorsed by 214 people" is verifiable at launch; "across 31 towns" is not, and a statement whose own first number is soft undercuts the evidence posture. Normalization — joining "Hamilton", "hamilton ont" and "The Hammer" — happens later, by code or by hand, and unlocks the By-town view and the Atlas linkage.

### 7.3 `data/meetups.json`

```json
{
  "id": "m-0007",
  "title": "Open bench night",
  "starts": "2026-09-04T19:00:00-04:00",
  "venue": "Welland Fabrication, 12 Ross St",
  "contact": "…",
  "calendar_url": "https://calendar.google.com/…"
}
```

### 7.4 Why the calendar link is not a data source

Three different things get called "a Google Calendar link":

| Kind | Parseable? |
|---|---|
| `render?action=TEMPLATE&text=…&dates=…&location=…` | **Yes** — fully self-describing in the query string |
| `event?eid=…` | No — opaque id, auth wall or empty shell server-side |
| `…/basic.ics` feed | It's a whole calendar, not an event |

**Decision: the four typed fields are the truth; the link is an optional destination we never parse.** This kills a whole class of parse failures, makes all three link types work, and — critically — lets an organizer with no calendar event at all still post a meetup. Requiring a calendar artifact is exactly the friction that stops an informal organizer from bothering. The form must not be more organized than the meeting.

## 8. Pipelines

Both reuse the pattern proven end to end in `patonhall/site` on 2026-08-18, and both are **approval-gated**.

```
public form → Google Form → Sheet → Apps Script
   → [Kit upsert + tag]        (endorsements only)
   → GitHub Issue (hidden data block)
   → `approved` label
   → GitHub Action → validate → write JSON → commit → close issue
```

### 8.1 Endorse form

Fields: **Name · Email · City / Town / Township · Trade / Expertise · Comment**.

The comment box is sized to invite a substantial post — this is a publication, not form data. **Cap 2,500 characters** (~400 words): generous enough for a proper letter, bounded so one 6,000-word paste cannot make the flip side unreadable for everyone after it.

### 8.2 Publishing notice

Exact copy, displayed above the submit button:

> **Comments are posted as written — we don't edit them. Please avoid defamatory or foul language.**

This is true under a review step and promises the thing people actually care about, which is not being edited.

### 8.3 The approval gate is an editorial desk

Publishing a named stranger's words under NA's banner — with no incorporated entity behind it — is a publishing decision, not spam triage. Two labels:

- `approved` — the endorsement joins the roster
- `publish-comment` — the comment text is written to the JSON

Both required for a comment to appear. This allows keeping someone on the roster while declining to publish a paragraph that is defamatory about a named local employer, abusive, or off-topic.

Bot filters run **before** the commit, because a public repo keeps anything published in history even after it is removed from the page: honeypot field, per-email rate limit, cap on links in the comment, slur blocklist. These reject bots, not opinions.

### 8.4 Add-a-meetup form

Fields: **Title · Date & time · Venue · Contact · Calendar link (optional)**. Same transport, same `approved` gate, no Kit side effect.

**On-page, not a new tab.** `＋ ADD` opens the form in place — a panel below the band, or the band expanding into one — and it posts to the Google Form in the background, exactly as the endorse form does. Rationale: the whole design intent of the band is that posting a meetup is a low-ceremony act an informal organizer will actually complete. Bouncing them to a Google-branded page in a new tab is the single highest-friction moment in the flow and the one most likely to lose them.

Both forms therefore share one submission module: build the POST body from named fields, fire it, handle the opaque response, show status inline. Write it once.

## 9. Stack

Static HTML/CSS with minimal JS. New markup and new design — the *mechanism* is borrowed from Paton Hall, the look is not (§2).

- No framework, no build step required at launch. If a shell/partial system is wanted later, follow `build.py`'s pattern: edit `src/`, commit generated output, never a runtime dependency.
- Typography and texture: editorial register, closer to Clark's field report than to Paton Hall's Excalidraw/Rough.js treatment. **No hand-drawn borders, no Open Color palette** — those are Paton Hall's identity.
- Desktop-first is acceptable; mobile must at minimum not break, since the statement is meant to be forwarded and read on a phone.
- Hosting: GitHub Pages from the repo root, `.nojekyll`, `CNAME` → `niagaraassembly.com`.
