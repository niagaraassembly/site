Why IPC

IPC standards are the common language of electronics manufacturing: they say what an acceptable solder joint, a soldered assembly or a wire harness looks like, so a buyer, a builder and an inspector can agree without arguing. The association behind them was founded in 1957 and, since 2025, is called the Global Electronics Association. It keeps the IPC name for its standards and certifications, so the credentials you hear about are still IPC ones.

EPTAC, a long-established North American provider, certifies to the IPC standards, and its Canadian range follows the same pattern we describe below: a set of standards, each with entry, trainer and expert levels. We use it as our reference. For the details of any course — content, length, schedule, fees — go to EPTAC's course pages or its 2026 Canadian schedule; we would rather point you to the source than restate it and get it wrong.

Where we begin

Introductory means the entry level of IPC's program, the Certified IPC Specialist. We plan to begin with one to three of these standards:

StandardWhat it coversWho it suits
IPC-A-610 Accept and reject criteria for electronic assemblies, shown as visual examples across the three product classes. Inspectors, quality staff, buyers, managers.
J-STD-001 Materials, methods and acceptable conditions for soldered electrical and electronic assemblies, with hands-on practice. Operators and technicians who solder.
IPC/WHMA-A-620 Requirements and acceptance for cable and wire harness assemblies. Harness builders and their inspectors.

Descriptions follow EPTAC's course list. Which of the three we open with depends on what people here ask for.

How IPC certification works

Certification is program-specific and has levels. Per IPC:

LevelWhat it means
CIS
Certified IPC Specialist		 The entry level, for operators, engineers and quality personnel who apply a standard. This is where we start.
CIT
Certified IPC Trainer		 Authorized to deliver CIS training and administer the exams.
MIT
Master IPC Trainer		 An optional designation for trainers working with an IPC Licensed Training Center.
CSE
Certified Standards Expert		 An advanced role: interpreting the standard, settling disputes and working with the standards committees.

The point that matters for us: only a certified trainer can grant an IPC certificate. Where a class is an IPC course, it is taught by someone IPC recognises, and the credential is IPC's. Where it is ours, it says so.

Stripped down, and enhanced

Two things are true at once. The certification track is the certification track; we do not water it down. Around it, we can offer more or less.

  • Stripped down — a focused course for someone who needs the credential and nothing else, to be delivered with our partners.
  • Enhanced — the same course with Clark Courses alongside it. No IPC credential attaches to those, and we label them plainly.

The education around the certificate

Passing an inspection standard tells you what good looks like on a bench. It does not tell you why the industry is shaped the way it is. The Clark layer is meant to. Topics we intend to build out:

  • How a design becomes a product: fabrication, assembly, test.
  • Supply chains: components, distribution, contract manufacturers and the customers they serve, and where this region fits.
  • The history of the industry and its standards.
  • Emerging technologies, and what they change on the shop floor.

A short history of the standards

WhenWhat happened
1957Founded as the Institute of Printed Circuits by six printed circuit board manufacturers.
1960sIPC-A-600 sets acceptability criteria for printed boards.
1977Renamed the Institute for Interconnecting and Packaging Electronic Circuits.
1980sIPC-A-610 defines acceptability for assembled boards, and the first worker certification program follows.
1999Adopts the name IPC.
2017Establishes the IPC Education Foundation.
2025Becomes the Global Electronics Association, keeping the IPC name for standards and certification.

Source: Global Electronics Association.
