Manufacturing training

Manufacturing is where we want to end up, and we are honest that it is further off than electronics. This page maps six areas of the work, shows where recognised training already exists, and says plainly where we need help to build the rest under Clark.

Aspirational. We do not currently offer any of the courses named here, and nothing on this page can be booked. Where we describe what we would do, we say “would” or “exploring”.

A different problem from electronics

Electronics has one standards body and one certification program, so a training path can be drawn from a single source. Manufacturing has no such thing. Instead there are many bodies, each owning a slice: trades, safety, maintenance, quality, machining, welding. What is missing is someone connecting the slices for a person in this region. That connecting work is the part we think Niagara Assembly and its Membership can do.

Industrial Automation

Controls, PLCs, robots and the people who keep them running. In Canada the trade routes are the Red Seal trades of Industrial Electrician and Industrial Mechanic (Millwright), through apprenticeship. On the credential side, ISA certifies control systems technicians (CCST) and automation professionals (CAP). Locally, Mohawk College teaches PLC programming and ladder logic in its Manufacturing Engineering Technician – Automation program.

Maintenance and Safety

SMRP's CMRT certifies maintenance and reliability technicians, and its CMRP covers the professional side. Safety has an extra layer: some Ontario workplace training, including Working at Heights, joint health and safety committee certification and WHMIS, must come from Ministry-approved providers. CSA Z460 is the Canadian standard for controlling hazardous energy, the lockout at the heart of machine safety. We would not replace approved providers; we would give them a room and an audience.

Quality and Production Systems

The MSSC Certified Production Technician covers four modules: safety, quality practices and measurement, manufacturing processes and production, and maintenance awareness. Hamilton's Mohawk College already pairs it with an early college program. For quality specialists, ASQ offers technician and Six Sigma credentials. The MSSC modules touch two of our six areas, so we are exploring them as a common foundation.

CNC and Digital Manufacturing

NIMS offers ANSI-accredited machining credentials, including CNC mill and lathe operation, earned through hands-on and theory tests. Software has its own credentials, such as Mastercam for CAM programming and SME for manufacturing technologists and additive manufacturing technicians. Machinist and Tool and Die Maker are Red Seal trades.

Fabrication Techniques

Welding in Canada is unusually formal. The CWB Group certifies welders, inspectors and supervisors, and for many structures a welder must be qualified by the CWB and work for a CWB-certified company. Welder is also a Red Seal trade. That means we cannot certify anyone casually, but we can connect people to those who can, and teach what surrounds it: reading drawings, fit-up, finishing, and working safely.

Emerging Technology

This is the area with no single map, and it is where we can do the most that is new. Additive manufacturing, robotics, digital twins and connected plants change faster than any curriculum. Public efforts exist, such as NGen, an industry-led non-profit with free membership that backs advanced manufacturing projects and additive manufacturing adoption in Canada. We would fill the gap with demos, talks, tours and launches through Clark, including the tools our own Membership is building.

Already here

We are not starting from an empty field, and we do not want to duplicate what works. On the Ontario side, Mohawk College teaches automation, CNC and computer-integrated manufacturing, with certifications such as SolidWorks Professional and FANUC Robotics Operation in its Mechanical Engineering Technology program. Across the river, the Northland Workforce Training Center runs paid immersion training in advanced manufacturing, and the NY Manufacturing Extension Partnership works on manufacturing workforce. Our job is to link these to people and shops who would not otherwise find them.

Where Clark fits

  • Convene. Host courses and approved providers, so a shop owner has one place to look.
  • Connect. Map a person's next step across the trades, safety, quality and machining pathways above.
  • Teach what nobody certifies. Short, practical Clark Courses on the everyday work between credentials, clearly labelled as ours.

What we need

None of this happens without people who know the work. If any of these is you, we would like to hear from you.

  • Tradespeople, machinists, welders, maintenance and quality people willing to teach, from an evening talk to a full course.
  • Shops that could host a class or let learners use a machine.
  • Approved safety and credential providers who want an audience in this region.
  • Employers willing to say which skills they cannot find.
  • Colleges and training bodies interested in working with us.
