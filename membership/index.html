Membership

Niagara Assembly is a members-led company.

Its purpose is to help its Members pioneer the workspaces, infrastructure and digital tools that lift all boats across the Niagara Peninsula — from Brantford and Port Dover through Hamilton, east across Haldimand to Fort Erie and Niagara Falls, and over the river into Erie and Niagara counties.

Read our Call for Infrastructures.

We are not a fund and we are not a chamber of commerce.

We are a lightweight collective, soon to be incorporated, that brings industrial operators, entrepreneurs, engineers, builders and other people interested in making things into useful relationships.

We do this through common spaces, sponsored stand-ups, special events, certified training and peer-led workshops — and by building the digital tools that make those connections useful between meetings.

We are a place to find other builders, and a place where building happens.

That might mean a small electronics solution for a local business. It might mean a robotics project, a new production system, a training program, a piece of software, or eventually something much larger.

Membership is participation in this work.

We want our spaces, our events and our programming to be places where people in the region find the future — and we need the ideas, experience, resources and assistance of Members to build them.

What membership makes possible

The Assembly is intended to grow according to what its Members actually need.

As Membership grows and self-organizing takes hold, we are testing products, services and shared infrastructure that can make the company sustainable while creating useful capacity across the region and beyond.

Some of the things we are working toward include:

  • Mobile electronics specialist training — delivered where the work is happening rather than where a classroom happens to be.
  • Real-estate and logistics visualization systems — the Industrial Atlas is the first of these, and is already public.
  • Integrated Production Environments — shared technical environments for building software and industrial systems, including our work with Theia.
  • Physical infrastructure — workshops, production space, laboratories, meeting places and other facilities that Members can develop and use together.
  • New tools and services — built around problems that Members encounter in the real world.

The principle is simple:

If something is useful to one Member, we should ask whether it can become useful infrastructure for many.

Who can become a Member?

There are three levels of Membership beyond the mailing list.

Regional

For individuals working in, around or alongside industry in the region.

Regional Members bring their knowledge, interests, projects and relationships into the Assembly. They are the people who make the network useful.

Company

For firms that want their people connected to the network and their capabilities visible to the wider Assembly.

Company Members can bring real problems to the community, find people and organizations capable of solving them, and help develop infrastructure that strengthens their own operations and the regional ecosystem at the same time.

Founding

A one-time founder's membership for people backing the vision for Niagara Assembly and its first space before it opens, with permanent recognition as one of the individuals who made the first space in downtown Hamilton possible.

Champions help create the conditions for new infrastructure, experiments and institutions to emerge before there is necessarily a conventional business case for them.

A different kind of membership

Membership is not simply a way of purchasing access.

It is how the company is governed, funded and built.

The Assembly is intended to become more useful as more Members participate in deciding what should exist, contributing to its development, and making use of what others have built.

This is an old idea.

The Mechanics’ Institutes of the nineteenth century were built around the proposition that useful knowledge and useful infrastructure could be organized collectively by the people who needed them.

We are interested in extending that principle into the present.

Join because there is something you want to build.

Bring a problem. Bring a project. Bring a capability. Bring a place. Bring an idea.

Then help us figure out what should exist next.
